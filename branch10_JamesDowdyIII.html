1(ii). James Dowdy III born April 1786 (birth calculated from death record). His father directed that son James get at least 2 years of schooling in his 1804 will - thus indicating James was still a young man in 1804. He served in the War of 1812 as a corporal under Capt Benjamin Allen, 17th Regt, Virginia Militia, and applied for a pension. He was age 63 in 1850 Cumberland Co VA census, age 73 in 1860 census. He died 2 June 1860 age 73y 2m 15d at Cumberland VA. His widow's pension statement asserted that James Dowdy died 24th June 1860 (a later document says 22nd, an 1873 Chancery document says 25th).

1811 Cumberland County Deed - This Indenture 7 November 1811 between Frances Dowdy relict of James Dowdy, deceased, Richard Dowdy, James Dowdy, and Jesse Dowdy, sons of said deceased, all of the county of Cumberland of the one part and Jacob Bransford of the same county of the other part... for the consideration of the sum of seven hundred eighty two dollars thirty four cents ... a certain tract of land lying in the aforesaid county of Cumberland, containing two hundred twenty nine acres be the same more or less, and bounded by the lands of the said Jacob Bransford, John Colquitt, Noton Goodman, little Guinea creek and the land of Lewis Isbell, it being the same the said James Dowdy, deceased, possessed of and where on the said Frances now resides

Will of James Dowdy [annexed to the Chancery lawsuit of Cumberland Co VA, documents filed 1860, suit dated 1873] - In the name of God Amen. I James Dowdy of the county of Cumberland being of sound mind and disposing memory knowing the uncertainty of life and wishing to provide for the disposition of the little estate I may leave at my death do make nd ordain this to be my last will and testamint herby revoking all former wills by me made. First I desire as soon after my decease as may be consistant with the interest of my estate all my just debts and funeral expenses be paid, this being done, the ballance of my estate both real and person (consisting of whatever it may) with the exception of a negro girl named Patty now in the possession of my daughter Amanda J Agee, upon [ ] an herinafter noticed, I lend to my beloved wife Martha for and during her normal life in widowhood to be used and managed by her (uncontroled by my executor herinafter named) as she may find most condusive to her comfort, fully belieivng that she will do full justice to her children to the extent of her ability. To my son Thomas Dowdy in trust for the use and benefit of my daughter Amanda J Agee and her children I give the negro girl Patty herinbefore named at the value of six hundred dollars to be managed by the said trustee as he may find it most to the interest and comfort of the said Amanda, in no manner however to be liable fo the debts or contracts of her husband Lem E Agee. I also give to the said Thomas Dowdy in trust as aforesaid and under the like limintations and restrictions as the girl Patty, a negro boy Daniel at the value of seven hundred dollars, not to take effect however desiring the life or widowhood of my beloved wife, to whom Daniel is loaned as part of my estate, and in the event of the death of Daniel dbefore possession by the said trustee, his value is to be supplied by another negro man or boy of equal value to be taken out of general estate before division as herinafter provided. It is my will and desire that the property specifically given in trust for the benefit of Amanda J Agee as well as the portion of my estate which will fall to her share in the final distribution of my estate as he might provide shall pass in fee to the children of the said Amanda or to the descendants of such as may have died leaving children. It is my will and desire that at the death of my beloved wife Martha my estate hereinbefore loaned to her for life shall be divided as follows. To my two sons Marshall and Wilson Dowdy, I give one hundred and fifty acres of land the line to commence ont eh Davenport Road at a point so as by running directly [ ] the plantation to include the mansion house and appertenances. on the side of the one hundred and fifty acres, the balance of my land I give to my three other chilren to wit, Thomas and James Dowdy and Amanda J Agee. My personal estate I desire may be divided into five equal parts giving to each of my said children one fifth part hereof, to be held as herin proved that is to say the portion of my estate to which my daughter Amanda J Agee may be entitled to in the general division of my estate I desire may be placed i the hands of Thomas Dowdy in trust for the benefit of the said Amanda and her children to be held by the said trustee under the same restrictions and limitations as the slaves hereinbefore specifically given are held. In the general division of my estate the portion to which Amanda J Agee may be entitled I desire may be charged with the slaves Patty and Daniel at the value herin stipulated but not with Patty's increase (if any) I also desire that the portion of my estate to which my son James may be entitled in the division therof may be held in trust by my son Thomas for the use and support of my son James so as not to be liable for his debts or contracts but that the profits therof may go to the support of his family and the remainder at his death. Lastly I appoint my son Thomas Dowdy executor of this my last will and testament and request that he may be permitted to qualify as such without giving security. In witness whereof I have herunto set my hand and affixed my seal this 6th day of February 1860 <s> James Dowdy. Signed and sealed in presence of A S Ford, C. R Raimore

married Martha Smith on 18 April 1822 in Cumberland County, by Joseph Jenkins. This marriage is identiied in the War of 1812 pension records. Records say James was not married prior to this marriage. James 'always lived at his house in Cumberland County and she removed from her father's house to live at his house."; She is enumerated in 1850 census, age 40 but this age must be wrong (should be 50?). Age 56 in 1860 census (born 1804); Identified as the wife of James Dowdy in her son Thomas P Dowdy's Chancery suit of 1873; In her answer to the Chancery suit dated 17 December 1860, she agreed that the slaves of her late husband and his land be divided amongst the children: She identified the slaves viz George, Robert, Isaah, Fanny and her child, and John, Scott, Peggy, Daniel and Jim; She applied for a widow's pension in 1881 based on the War of 1812 service of her deceased husband James Dowdy.

This family is confirmed correct via a newspaper obituary of his wife Mary who died in Idaho)
