1842 Oct 13 - Will of William Dowdy, In the name of God Amen I William Dowdy sen. of the County of Bedford and State of Virginia being weak in body but of sound and perfect mind and memory blessed be Almighty God for the same do make and publish this my last will and testament in manor and form following that is to say First I give and bequeath unto my beloved wife Elizabeth all my land being and lying on the west side of the Dumplin mountain road and adjoining Jacob Shepherd Hundley Dowdy and others withthe appertainnances thereto during her life time not to be destroyed willfully so as to injure the sle of said land at the expiration of her time, excluding her from any lot or part of the balance of my lands. I do also give and bequeath unto my beloved son Cary A. all my lands except that portion alloted to my wife in free and full possession so as to make a crop without interruption after my death for and in consideration of the improvements made by him on said land after his crop if finished I wish for said land to be sold at the discretion of my executors and 50 dollar given out of the proceeds to each of my beloved sons. The balance of which I want equally divided between each of my children. I also allot to my beloved daughter Suey one cow and feather bed and furniture to make her equal with my other daughters donations. I also wish for all the balance of my property to be sold and after myjust debts is paid and my beloved wifes thirds taken out to be equally divided amongst each of my children that part going to my daughter Pheniba who intermarried with John Drewry to go to her and to the heirs of her body forever likewise my daughter Ann who intermarried with Michael Crotty to go to her and the heirs of her body forever. 1938 Sept 30 Dickson County (TN) Herald newspaper (article published in Historical Sketches of Dickson Co TN by Jill K. Garrett, 1971)

Jessie A. Dowdy of Sylvia TN (Dickson Co)

"...I was born March 6, 1854 on a farm in Bedford county, Virginia, the son of William and Martha Hudson Dowdy. . The whole family of four boys and seven girls was born there. ONly one besides myself is living, a sister, Mrs. Sarah Frazier of Nashville.

I never went to school a day in my life. I don't know one letter from another and if I did I couldn't see it now. When I was young times were hard. They call the times hard now, but theynare not like they were then. When a man had such a family as my father did and only one to work, he was up against it. I have deformed feet and never worked at all while we lived in Virginia. I was thirteen years old when we moved from there to Christian county , Kentucky, to another farm. We went on a train, for we didn't have any wagon. We didn't have anything but necessities.

I can remember a little about the Civil War, the squads of men, etc. but I wasn't old enough to remember much. We were living in Virginia at the time and times were awful hard.

We didn't stay in Kentucky very long. In 1881 we came to Montgomery county, Tennessee, and lived on a farm in the neighborhood of Clarksville. I had been there two years when I was married to Nettie Brown. We had four children. Three of them are living and I can't tell about the other. He may be living and he may be dead. He has been away from home and I haven't heard from him in fifteen years. His name is Irvin. The others are Mrs. Minnie Smith of Cheatham county, Mrs. Alma Henly of Montgomery county, and Allen Dowdy of Sylvia. I have 13 grandhcilfdren and two great-grandchildren.

Nettie died on Labor Day in 1913. We had been to a picnic and while coming home the horse ran away with us in the buggy. She lived about two and a half hours afterwards. Four months after she died I married Ella Brock of Dickson, having just come here to live. I bought this place and have been here since and have bought more land around it. I have 151 acres on the White Forks of Yellow Creek and sixty acres here.

I am a member of the Church of Christ and I feel like I am doing the best I know and that's all I can say, let it be right or wrong. Dont' any of us know. We'll have to find out later.

I am in good health, but I can't see good, for I'm almost blind. I wear a pair of glasses that are not much good. I hear all right and I feel just as well as I ever felt in my life. Being crippled has hindered me all my life from abyhood on and old age mekes me no account now.

Ella and I live here alone. I don't work, don't do anything. I havent' been outside the yard at night that I know of in three years. They have been holding two big meetings here but I haven't been to any of them..."
