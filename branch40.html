Thomas Dowdy Sr.
Branch #40 - of Deep River Chatham NC

For decades, researchers have connected the Dowdy family of Deep River in southern Chatham NC to another Thomas Dowdy family of Rocky River, in northern Chatham NC. The latter migrated to North Carolina from Sussex Virginia. However, there is clear evidence that these two families are distinct and separate. While it is likely the two families are related, no evidence of that link is known.

This Lineage Page Last Updated February 2024
Thomas Dowdy Sr. born about 1733 (estimated by birth of dau. Lucy); First appeared briefly in the 1753 Sussex VA Albemarle Parish registry with first wife Elizabeth Parsons. Moved to the southern part of Orange County, later Chatham County NC along the waters of Deep River by 1763; Thomas Dowdy of Chatham NC gave material aid and a gun to the revolutionary cause. He also served as a soldier in the NC militia. The accounts of the auditors of Hillsborough District, NC, in custody of the North Carolina Historical Commission, show payments for sundries furnished. The Treasurer's and Comptroller's papers, Revolutionary Army Accounts, Vol. XI, p.50, folio 3, in the North Carolina Archives, show pay vouchers for Thomas Dowdy that were paid in 1783-84. He died before Feb 1815; Will dated 1805 and proven in Chatham NC court in 1815.

1805 Chatham Co NC Original Wills Box CR.022.801.8 - Will of Thomas Dowdy Sr, Chatham Co NC - In the name of God amen. I Thomas Dowdy sen'r being in perfect sences and memory do constitute this my last will and testament in the following manner (to wit) In the first place I give and bequeath my soul in to the hands of the Almighty God who give it and my body to be buried in a Christian and decent manner. I then dispose of my property in the following manner. I give unto my daughter Nelly Goodman one dollar. i also give unto my daughter Lucy Dowdy one dollar. Item. I give unto my daughter Sarah Dowdy one dollar. Item. I give unto my son John Dowdy one dollar. Item I give unot my son William Dowdy one dollar. Item. I give unto my son Thomas Dowdy one dollar. Item. I give unto my son James Dowdy one dollar. I give unto my son Armsted Dowdy one dollar. Item. I give unto my son Joab Dowdy one mare, bed and furniture. Item. I give unto my son Benjamin Dowdy one horse bed and furniture. Item. i give unto my son Balaam Dowdy one dollar. Item I give unto my daughter Rebecca Dowdy one mare bed and furniture. All the rest of my property consisting of lands and tenaments and all my house hold and kitchen furniture I give unto my beloved wife Rebecca Dowdy [during] her life time and after her death it is my desire that it should all be equally divided amongst the following persons Thomas Dowdy, James Dowdy, Armsted Dowdy, Balaam Dowdy, Joab Dowdy, Benjamin Dowdy and Rebecca Dowdy. The above I constitute my last will and testament this 27th of January in the year of our Lord one thousand eight hundred and five <his mark> Thomas Dowdy. Signed sealed and delivered in the presence of Test: Jno McIntosh, Chiles McGee; Proven in Chatham court February session 1815 by John McIntosh

Critical to the lineage theory presented on this page is the belief that the above Will for Thomas Dowdy appears to differentiate between children of an unnamed 1st wife, viz: Nelly, Lucy, Sarah, John and William, and children of the 2nd wife Rebecca, viz: Thomas, James, Armstead, Balaam, Joab, Benjamin and Rebecca. Identifying Elizabeth Parsons as this Thomas Dowdy's 1st wife finally solves various research problems caused by several Chatham deed and court records. These records have perplexed Dowdy researchers for a decade. It is not proof, however, but it's workable.

married 1) Elizabeth Parsons, daughter of Robert Parsons of Sussex Co Virginia; she died sometime before the birth of Thomas Dowdy Jr, approximately in 1774 .

13 May 1798 Thomas Dowdy, Senior, gave Power of Attorney to his son William Dowdy, “to sue for, recover & receive all sums of money and dower rights of and from the Estate of Robert Parsons, deceased, whose daughter, Elizabeth, I married.” . Witnesses: M. McKenzie & J. McBride. Proven at Chatham NC May term of court 1798

Note: A Robert Parsons left a Will in Sussex Co VA proven there 18 Sept 1777 (WB C pg 265) His children and grandchildren were named. Dau: Mary Sanders, Uriah Weathers, Amey Ekman; Sons: Wm Parsons , Henry Parsons; Grandchildren: Osburn Smith, son of dau Amy Ekman, Robert Parsons son of Henry Parsons; Likely son in laws named: Isaac Weathers, John Sanders, Adam Ekman; Wit: Robt Watson, Nicholas Ogburn, Thos Whitfield; NO MENTION OF DAUGHTER ELIZABETH DOWDY. The above will of Robert Parsons was probated 21 years prior to the Thomas Dowdy's attempt in 1798 to recover his wife's estate. (this is suspicious) The wife named as Eleanor at the 1746 birth of daughter Amy Parsons, Henry in 1749, died 9 Nov 1770 "wife of Robt" (Albemarle Parish Reg), so there would not be a delay in distribution of the dower portion of Parson's estate, so 21 years is problematic Why was Elizabeth Dowdy not named in her father's Will. Both Robert and Eleanor Parsons were godparents of Lucy Dowdy, so not likely there is another Robert Parsons, father of Elizabeth.

1753 January 7. Sussex Co VA - Albemarle Parish Register - Lucy Dowdy daughter of Thomas Dowdy and wife Elizabeth born. Robert Parsons, Elinore Parsons, and Elizabeth Askins godparents.

married 2) Rebecca _____ (surname likely Poe) she likely died sometime before 1818 when son Thomas Dowdy was granted administration bond for the estate of his father Thomas Dowdy, dec'd. and certainly by 1820 when the children began the process of selling their father's land as legatees of the estate.

Newspaper article from July 1930 - “Poe Reunion Recalls Famous Man: Gathering at Pittsboro This year Outstanding for Heroic Tales about Jesse, Who Left Virginia to Come to Chatham County in Revolutionary Days” by Myrtle Ellen LaBarr

"...Nor have I yet been able to learn the given name of his [Jesse's] father or mother, though the latter may possibly be available in some tradition of the Dowdy family. His mother later married a man of that name, by whom this first Jesse Poe had a half brother Thomas Dowdy, long a leader in May’s chapel church, as shown by Purefoy’s old History of the Sandy Creek Baptist association, published about 1858...."

Note: Jesse Poe featured in the 1930 news story was of a similar generation as Thomas Dowdy Jr (see below). Poe died February 15, 1859. And Thomas Dowdy Jr (next) is indeed the person referenced in the Sandy Creek Baptist publication. Supposedly, a young Jesse, his widowed mother, and step-brother Benjamin Poe b 1749, later a revolutionary war solider, migrated from Culpepper VA to Chatham where his mother supposedly married Dowdy. Benjamin Poe's pension record shows he enlisted in Chatham NC; therefore, the family was in Chatham before the Revolution (and the widowed mother could have married Thomas Dowdy and borne their first child, Thomas Dowdy, Jr. in 1774)

1(viii). Benjamin Dowdy born about 1780 in Chatham County NC; likely migrated to Georgia by 1800 census; Benjamin moved to North Georgia with his brothers and cousins. In 1810 he purchased 50 acres of land in Jackson County GA. He appears as a tax defaulter in 1818 and 1820 next door Franklin County GA and appears in the 1820 Jackson County census with 2 sons age 0-10. However, he apparently returned to Chatham County appearing in Chatham Co court road orders in 1826-7 and the 1830 Chatham Co NC census (age 40-50) with a younger wife (age 20-30), one male child (age 0-5) and one female child (age 0-5). He does not appear in the 1840 census for Chatham NC. He may be the Benjamin who appeared in Henderson Co TN before 1840, appearing in that census age 50-60 with 3 sons viz: ages 0-5, 5-10, and 10-15; with 3 daughters viz: 0-5 and 2 aged 5-10. His wife was age 30-40. She apparently died before 1850. The names of children in the 1850 Henderson TN census hints of the connection to the Chatham NC Benjamin Dowdy
