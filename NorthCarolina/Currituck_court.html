1887 Baum and Others vs. Currituck Shooting Club
The Southeastern Reporter p 673.

Deposition - Josephus Baum testified as follows: "I knew Willoughby Dowdy; knew him as long ago as forty years. He lived on the west side of the sound. He never lived on the beach. I knew the boundaries of the Willoughby Dowdy land as described in his deed to Samuel Cooper. I knew that its boundaries embraced the land in the plat. 1 know where the Thos. Payner line is, and also where the Thos. Dowdy land is. Old persons which are dead have told me where the Poyner line was. It is correct as stated in the plat. I knew the Thos. Dowdy line. I am fifty years old. I have known the land ever since 1840. It is on the banks lying between the sea and Beasley's bay. It has been used for gunning and grazing. It is all it is fit for. The sand has nearly covered it all. A part of it was cultivated twenty or twenty-five years ago by Wm. Dowdy, a tenant of Willoughby Dowdy. Afterwards Samuel Cooper cultivated it one or two years, and then Abram Baum about three. It was cultivated in all for eight years. About one-third of the whole tract was covered with myrtle. It is all now a bald beach, and has not been fit for cultivation for twenty-five years. When I first knew it, a third could have been cultivated. It is valuable for shooting. I rented it out several years, from 1855 to the war, but not during the war. I did not live on it during the war. After the war I leased it out until 1869. In 1869 the administrator of Jacob Bauin rented an undivided half to the Currituc'k Shooting Club. I have seen them occupying it up to this spring from that time."

Deposition - Abram Baum testified as follows: "I have known the lands for fifty-five years. I know where Thos. Poyner's line was. My line was the same as Thos. Dowdy's line. Thos. Poyner showed me that line fifty-five years ago. A house was built on the Willoughby Dowdy tract about fifty-two years ago. Willoughby Dowdy built it. It has been moved away for about thirty years. Willoughby Dowdy rented the land three years to Wm. Dowdy. Cooper cultivated it three years. This land was generally known as the Cooper and Willougliby Dowdy land. For twenty-five years the sand has covered the land, and there has been no fence around it. There was no fence. Anybody's cattle could run on it. There has been a box there within the last fifteen years. The Currituck Shooting Club has a box there. Several persons claimed it, but I don't know how. This is the same land as is described in Samuel Cooper's deed to ?. ?. Chaplain, and in ?. ?. Chaplain's deed to Jos. Baum. The land covered by the deed of Caleb Dowdy and Sarah Dowdy is south of the Willoughby Dowdy land. Abram Baum lived on it at the date of my father's will. The club-house is now situated on it. Caleb and Thos. Dowdy's lines are the same. No one cultivated the land in question [the Willoughby Dowdy land] after the house was removed from it, except Abram Baum, who cultivated it subsequently for about three years. That he was present at the division of the Dowdy land. So was Mr. Poyner and some others. At that time Mr. Poyner showed me the S. line of his land, which comes down to Glade creek, and was the north line of the Willoughby Dowdy
land. He [Baum] afterwards bought the Thomas Dowdy land, and recognized the division line of the Dowdys. Thos. Dowdy's share was 150 acres, and Willoughby Dowdy's share was equally as large. It extended from Thos. Poyner's, on the north, to my line, [the Thos. Dowdy line,] on the south. Since I first knew the land, there has been large accretions to it by the gradual filling up of the water on the bay, as much as fifty acres. The Caleb Dowdy and Thos. Dowdy line is the same as the line of myself, and now the Currituck Shooting Club. After my father bought the land he removed the house, and I cultivated about20 acres, where the house had stood, about three years. Afterwards my father used the land for grazing his cattle until his death. There never was a fence around the land. Then Josephus Baum used it in the same way, and also he rented it out to gunners until the war. The
widow of Jacob Baum rented out the interest of Jacob Baum in the same."
