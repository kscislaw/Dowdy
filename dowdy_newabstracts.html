                                    And the will, and exhibits, the court doth decree that the said Executors deliver to the said Frances Dowdy, the widow, all the slaves and other personal                                                 property of the said testator, except what shall be otherwise appropriate by this decree, upon condition however that the said widow enter into bond of good                                         security to be judged of by the court in a sufficient penalty to the said executor, to refund the said property to the executors in case any debts or future claims or                                     demands shall come against the said executors or estate of said testator, and the court doth further order and decree that the said Executors deliver to the said                                         Richard Dowdy, guardian of William Dowdy, the slave Morning and his increase, to hold as the property of said infant upon condition that the said guardian                                             enters into bond of security as aforesaid to refund as aforesaid in the case of the widow to the executors if required.  It appeared to the court that the said mare                                     colt was delivered to James, by his confession in court, and the said testator Negro Funny was delivered of a child, which was delivered to Jesse Dowdy the                                         nephew mentioned in the bill and will.  It seems to the Court that the bill as to Thomas infant, need not to be acted on as he the infant has no present interest in                                         the estate, or any interest that can be affected in this suit and this court is continued for the report of the commissioners.

1811 Cumberland County Deed

This Indenture made this seventh day of November one thousand eight hundred and eleven between Frances Dowdy (relict of James Dowdy, deceased, Richard Dowdy, James Dowdy, and Jesse Dowdy, sons of said deceased, all of the county of Cumberland of the one part and Jacob Bransford of the same county of the other part witnesseth that the said Frances, Richard, James and Jesse, for the consideration of the sum of seven hundred eighty two dollars thirty four cents lawful money of Virginia to them in hand paid by the said Jacob, the receipt of which is hereby acknowledged, hath bargained, sold , and confirmed and by these present do absolutely bargain, sell, and confirm unto the said Jacob Bransford and to his heirs and assigns forever, a certain tract of land lying in the aforesaid county of Cumberland, containing two hundred twenty nine acres be the same more or less, and bounded by the lands of the said Jacob Bransford, John Colquitt, Noton Goodman, little Guinea creek and the land of Lewis Isbell, it being the same the said James Dowdy, deceased, possessed of and where on the said Frances now resides to have and to hold, the said sold land with all the privileges and appurtenances there unto belonging or in any wise appertaining complete possession of said sold lands and to be given unto this said Jacob, his heirs and assigns, on or before the twenty fifth day of December, one thousand eight hundred and twelve with the privilege of sowing grain and fallowing land any time after first day of October one thousand eight hundred and twelve and this said Frances, Richard, and Jesse for themselves and their heirs and assigns do covenant and agree with the said Jacob to warrant and forever defend the right and title of said sold lands and against the claim or demand of all persons or any person whatsoever unto the said Jacob and to his heirs an assigns forever in testimony whereof the said Frances, Richard, James, and Jesse hath set their hand and affixed their seals the day and date above written.

 

Witnesses Present:

Benjamin Fuqua                                                            Frances (her X mark) Dowdy.…seal

Noton Goodman                                                          Richard Dowdy……….………..seal

Sam C. Hatcher                                                            James Dowdy…………...……..seal

                                                                                    Jesse Dowdy……..…….………seal

 

Charle A. Ballow

William Stokes           for T. Dowdy

Ben Hobson

 

Cumberland November Court 1811.  This Deed was exhibited in Court and proved by Benjamin Fuqua and Sam Hatcher as to Frances Dowdy and ordered to be recorded as to her and continued for further proof as to the rest.

 

 

(From the margin, this deed delivered to Jacob Bransford March 20, 1815)

1825 Cumberland June Court 27th - Albert Jacob Dowdy and Decha Mosby Dowdy (Plaintiffs) against Anthony W. Crenshaw (Defendant) On the motion of the plaintiffs to set aside the order for binding them out, after argument the same is overruled, and it is ordered that the said plaintiffs return to the said Crenshaw and to stand bound by the Indenture’s binding to said Crenshaw and the court orders that the said Crenshaw keep Albert the elder either at school or the carpenter’s trade until he become Twenty one years of age.

 

1828 July 28, Chancery Court - Record On motion, ordered Anthony Crenshaw to appear to me and to show cause why the indentures of Albert Jacob Dowdy and Poindexter Dowdy, his apprentices, should not be revoked and why they should not be apprenticed to some other person.
