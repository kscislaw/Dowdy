ROBERT WINGENUND DOWDY Major, U. S. Army, Retired - Son of Joseph F. and Mary J. Dowdy (married Oct. 5, 1845, Marshall County, Miss.—Found in Holly Springs Court House) was born in Marshall County, Mississippi January 31, 1854. His paternal grandfather, William Dowdy, of Scotch-Irish descent, was an extensive planter in Marshall County, and was a man mostly self-made of strong and rugged character whose influence was felt throughout the community. His maternal grandfather was Richard Puckett, who was a type of the refined and cultured gentlemen of the South of three-quarters of a century ego. A man beloved in South Alabama, North Mississippi and West Tennessee. In 1856 his father moved to Pocahontas, Tenn., where the family lived until after the war was declared, 1861. At the close of the war, when everything had been lost, his father moved to Memphis, Tenn., and engaged in the commission business. While there the children were sent to private schools, and in 1871 the subject of this sketch was sent to Georgetown College, D. C. In 1873 his health broke down and he was sent to one of his father's plantations in Arkansas to recuperate. After he had been there a year and more his father wrote him that it was time to decide what he was to do in life, and that he would allow him three months to consider, and at the expiration of that time to come home and report. He reported at the time stated. After the affectionate salutations his father said, "My son, have you decided what occupation you would rather engage in?" The son answered, "Yes, sir. Father, I know how you stood by the Confederate States, and what your memories and sentiments are, and mine are the same as yours, if you can consent, I believe I would like to go to West Point and become an officer of the Army." The reply was characteristic of the true American, the true Confederate—which was, "My son, we now have but one flag; it is as much your duty to uphold it as that of any man north of Mason's and Dixon's line. If you will uphold it with honor to your country and credit to your family I shall be most happy." So he asked for the appointment and received, and entered the U. S. Military Academy as a cadet from the 1st District of Arkansas, July 1st, l875. He was graduated June 12th, 1879, and assigned as 2nd Lieut., 17th U. S. Infantry. He served with that regiment in Dacotah Territory until 1883. In June, 1882, he was married to Miss Annie Hamilton Clarkson of St. Louis, Mo., grand-daughter of Governor Archibald Gamble of that state. The issue of this union was two daughters, Katherine Mary and Ina Hamilton Dowdy. He was in command of Indian Scouts at Fort Gates, Dakotah from June 1880 to June 1883, when he was detailed as Professor of Military Science and Tactics at the University of the South, Sewanee, Tennessee. He was relieved from this duty August 15, 1886 and rejoined his regiment at Fort Russell, Wyoming. There he performed the usual garrison duties, with many excursions into the field, until 1892, when he was detailed as Professor of Military Science and Tactics at the University of Arkansas. In April, 1894, he was ordered to rejoin his regiment at Fort Russell, Wyoming and was appointed Regimental Quartermaster, which office he held for the stipulated period of four years. When the Spanish American War was declared he went to Cuba as 1st Lieut., Co. "B", 17th Infantry, but as the Captain was detailed as Major, he commanded the Company in all engagements until July 10th, when he received orders placing him on the retired list. He thereupon relinquished command and proceeded home. Having done full duty in Cuba and receiving the highest commendation of his Regimental and Brigade Commanders, Congress by special act restored him to the active list. Served in the Philippine Islands from Dec. 1899 to February 1902. Retired from active service, with the rank of Major, July 10, 1903. Assigned to duty with the Mississippi National Guard, January 4, 1904. Last duty with National Guards of Arkansas, 1906. The Guards received the highest nerds ever given at the following manoeuvres. Died in Colorado Springs Klackmer Sanitarium, March 23, 1912. Buried in the National Cemetery in Little Rock, Arkansas. Cemetery Lot No. 6127. Buried by the side of his first wife, Annie Clarkson, daughter of Col. Charles Clarkson. Married Sera Searight of Austin, Texas, June 29, 1901, in Philippine Islands.
