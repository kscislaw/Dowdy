The Dowdy surname is widely accepted to be a spelling variation from the Irish "O'Dubhda", lords of the barony of Tireragh in County Sligo. Although there is no known proven genealogical link of present day Dowdy families to the ancient O'Dubhdas, or O'Dowdas, the surname spelling appears many times in ancient documents of clan family members. Descendants of the clan O'Dowda have for the most part anglicized their name to O'Dowd or Dowd.

The Dowdy surname appears somewhat rarely in Ireland lastly appearing in 17th century Irish documents. The name largely disappears from Ireland following that period. This is the period many of that name begin appearing in colonial Virginia and North Carolina. The dearth of evidence in Ireland may, however, result from the widescale destruction of Irish genealogical records in the 1920s rather than implying evidence of the origns of the American Dowdys.

While most American researchers accept the Irish origns of their families, one cannot ignore the large occurance of the surname in England. The IGI lists numerous Dowdy families in multiple English counties beginning in the 16th century. It is not known whether the English Dowdys are variations of the more common English Dawdy surname and/or whether those families were originally part of the Irish O'Dowda clan having immigrated at some point to England in the 16th century or earlier.
